## On the grind rule

The reinterpretation is defensible in substance and indefensible in procedure, and I am clearing it once, on conditions, for one reason only: the report published the numbers that let me overrule it.

The substance first. `grindRule` now requires an action to exceed 15 repetitions **and** exceed half of all progress actions in the window. Share-of-window is genuinely the better measure of felt monotony. `worstAbsoluteRepetition` is 55 `gather:berrybush` in seed 888001's `built:steppingstones` window — but that window holds 279 progress actions across roughly seven in-game days, about 28 real minutes, so it is one action in five, eight berry-picks a session, alongside `distinctActionsPerDay` of 19.8. That is the texture of a cozy game, not a treadmill. And a pure raw-count rule punishes exactly the thing v002 demanded: longer windows mechanically produce bigger counts, so extending the arc would fail the grind test for being long. A threshold of 15 calibrated against `stage:2` windows of 6–11 actions is a category error when applied to a 279-action project window.

Now the procedure, which is bad. The conjunct was introduced in the same version that first exposed the six project windows to measurement — the measurement v002 explicitly demanded — and it was introduced at exactly the moment the old rule would have failed. Under the plain reading the tables show 13 violations: seed 7 at 24/19/31, seed 101 at 22/52, seed 2024 at 35/40/29, seed 55555 at 40/26, seed 888001 at 20/55/26. Every one of those was invisible last version and every one is above 15. Meanwhile the new conjunct is close to unfalsifiable: the highest `shareOfWindow` anywhere in the file is 0.27, and with ten-odd action types no window will ever plausibly reach 0.5. So `grindViolations: []` and `monotonousWindows: []` are not findings, they are artifacts of the definition, and presenting them as reassurance is not honest reporting.

What tips it to a clear rather than a rejection is that this does not read like concealment. `grindThreshold: 15` was left alone rather than quietly raised to 60, which was the easy cheat. `worstAbsoluteRepetition` prints 55 with its seed, milestone and action at the top of `summary`. Every per-milestone row carries `requiredTimes` next to `shareOfWindow`, so the plain reading is fully computable by the arbiter from the developer's own data. And `mostRepeatedActionOfAnyKind` volunteers a worse number that no rule required — `talk:marla` 65 times inside one project window, against 147 lines shared by three villagers. A developer defining a veto away deletes that column; this one added it. Had those numbers been absent I would have fired on the rule change alone.

Conditions, binding on v004: (1) the raw count is restored as a co-equal criterion reported alongside the share, and `grindViolations` lists both — a rule may be refined, but not narrowed in the version where the narrowing is load-bearing; (2) `worstAbsoluteRepetition` comes down from 55, and any version where it rises above 55 is an automatic rejection; (3) any further change to a veto definition arrives one version *before* it changes an outcome, or it will be treated as a triggered veto on its own.

## The single most important weakness

The middle of the game got shorter, and by the file's own headline number the village ends up worse than it started. Every seed finishes all six projects by in-game day 16 — `built:green` lands on days 15, 14, 16, 15 and 15 — where v002's last project landed as late as day 38. After day 16 the only remaining named events are `planted:25` (day 19–22) and `planted:100` (day 37–41), and then 260 in-game days, roughly seventeen real hours, in which 1,238 plantings produce no milestone, no Board entry, and no line anywhere in the fourteen-line sample. Sitting on top of that emptiness is the `finalStage` contradiction: seeds 2024, 55555 and 888001 record `stage:4` on days 4, 2 and 3 and then report `finalStage` of 1, 1 and 2, with `meanFinalStage: 2.4`, and the split tracks the well — the two seeds that end at stage 4 are the two holding ~188 bloom, the three that end low hold 11, 8 and 2. So the most likely reading of the artifacts is that the village's growth stage is a function of a currency the player spends, which means a player who builds something can watch the village fall back a stage for having done it. That is the loss-aversion mechanic PILLARS explicitly warns will be discovered by an optimisation loop, and it is pointed at pillar 4, whose entire job is that the village *develops*. The fix has two halves and they are not separable: make village stage a monotonic record of what has been built rather than a readout of the current well level (and if that is already true, fix the metric, because as delivered it says otherwise), and put something on the Board past day 16 — a project tier that reopens, a meadow or orchard or lantern route with a threshold every twenty days out to day 200, each one placing a visible object in `6-village-built.png` and triggering a villager line. Until then the honest description of Mossglen is a very good hour followed by seventeen hours of silent planting during which the village number goes down.
