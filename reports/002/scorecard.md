## Verdict
ACCEPTED. No veto fires, and the two things v001 said had to move both moved substantially: the economy went from an overspill factor of 277.8 and 3,958.6 items rotting in the bag to 2.4 and 16, and the content arc went from finishing on in-game day 3 to producing new named milestones out to day 30–40 — roughly twelve real minutes of game becoming roughly two and a quarter hours. Critically, that stretch was *not* bought with repetition: the worst required repetition is unchanged at 13 and the median fell from 13 to 8, so the arc was extended with content rather than padded with cost, which is the correct way to have done it. The acceptance is real but not clean: visual charm is the one axis that did not move at all, and it did not move because nothing named in v001's scorecard was touched.

## The single most important weakness
The game's endless activity is its least meaningful one, and its most beautiful objects still have nowhere to go. After the last named milestone (days 30–40 across seeds) the only thing left is planting, and every seed does about 1,600 of them: `plantings` runs 1,599–1,642, the final acknowledgment is `planted:100`, and the remaining ~1,500 produce no milestone, no Board entry, and no line anywhere in the fourteen-line sample. That channel is also where the material goes — inflow totals about 13,400 items per seed against an itemized outflow of about 9,950, a gap of roughly 3,400 items, or 26% of everything gathered, leaving the economy through a route the metrics do not itemize. So `itemsLeftUnusedAtEnd: 16` is honest about the bag but quiet about the hole. Meanwhile the two finds the game draws best are still barely used: glass bead runs 741 inflow against 59 outflow in seed 7 and 740 against 32 in seed 888001 — 92 to 96% waste, essentially unchanged from v001's 769 against 25 — and feather runs 566 against 66. `economyFlags.resourcesWithNoSink: []` does not catch this because it only tests for zero. The game already knows these are display objects: in `4-home-interior.png` the glass bead sits on a shelf, deliberately placed, one of only about five player-placed items in a home that `pacing` says is full on day 1. The fix is a place for treasures that keeps opening — a case, a mantel, a village cabinet that grows as it fills and that villagers remark on — so that the rarest finds have a destination and the back 260 days have something to be *for*, rather than 1,500 silent plantings absorbing the overflow.
