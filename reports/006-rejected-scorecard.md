## On the grind veto now firing

The developer asks whether restoring a veto that then fires should count as the version failing the veto or as the version fixing the instrument. **Neither, and I want this to be the last time the question is asked.**

**The principle: restoring an instrument is neutral. It earns no credit and it grants no amnesty.** What an arbiter judges is the behaviour the instrument reveals, never the instrument's history. If a newly honest measurement exposes genuinely bad behaviour, the version fails on the behaviour, and the fact that the behaviour is old is irrelevant — the rubric judges the current state of the game, not the delta. And symmetrically, a version does not fail merely because a field that used to read `[]` now reads `13`. Any other rule is unstable in an obvious direction. "A newly restored veto does not count" teaches this loop to suppress a veto and then restore it for credit in the version that needs cover. "Any firing veto rejects" teaches it never to restore a veto at all — which is precisely what v005 caught happening and correctly refused to accept. Judge the behaviour; the instrument's provenance is not evidence about the game.

**So: is there grind in v006?** Not on the honest reading, and the count cannot answer the question because it has no denominator. `grindThreshold: 15` is compared against a raw count in windows that range from 6 to 279 progress actions and from 1 to 7 in-game days. The file refutes its own instrument twice over. Seed 7's `built:pondlantern` window is 24 repetitions across 2 days — **12.0 per in-game day**. Seed 888001's `built:steppingstones` window is 55 repetitions across 7 days — **7.86 per in-game day**. The count says the 55 is more than twice as bad; the rate says the 24 is half again worse; and `worstAbsoluteRepetition`, the file's headline figure, is therefore reported on the *less* repetitive of the two. Every one of the 13 violations sits in a window of 2 or more in-game days and every single-day window passes, which tells you what the instrument is actually measuring: window length.

Reading the rate instead — which this version, to its credit, is the first to supply — the worst repetition anywhere in 1,500 simulated in-game days is **12.25 per in-game day** (seed 101, `donate:berries`, 49 times over 4 days), no single action ever exceeds **0.20** of the progress actions in its window, `monotonousWindows` is empty, and the player performs ~21 distinct actions per day. That is not a treadmill. **Veto clear**, and clear narrowly rather than comfortably.

**The rule I am fixing, binding on v007 and every version after it:**

> **Grind is a rate, not a count.** The veto fires if any of the following is true:
> **(a)** `mostRepeatedActionPerInGameDay` ≥ **15** — the rubric's own threshold, applied to a per-in-game-day rate instead of a raw count;
> **(b)** any single action exceeds **0.35** `shareOfWindow` in a window of at least 20 progress actions;
> **(c)** `monotonousWindows` is non-empty.
>
> Raw counts over windows of unequal length are context and gate nothing **in either direction**: they may not be cited to fail a version and they may not be cited to clear one. The threshold, the denominator and the window definition may not be changed by the developer without an arbiter recording the change in a scorecard; a version that changes any of the three without such a record fails this veto by default.

This is not a favour to v006 and it is not toothless. Applied to v004 it fires immediately: `talk:pim` at 290 repetitions in a 16-day window is ~18 per in-game day, over limb (a). Applied to v006 it clears at 12.25 with less than 20% of head-room. It is a gate that a real version in this series has already failed.

**One gap that the restored instrument does not close, and that nobody has yet noticed.** The grind windows are milestone windows, and every seed's `grind` object holds exactly nine of them — `stage:2` through `built:green` — all of which close by day 17 of 300. **The instrument therefore inspects about 5% of the run and is structurally blind to the other 95%.** In that unmeasured stretch the player performs 1,106 plantings, roughly 3.7 per in-game day for 283 days, and no field in the file evaluates it. This is not currently a violation on any reading, but it means the grind veto cannot see the part of the game most likely to become a treadmill. Any back-half content must emit milestones, or it ships into a blind spot.

## Verdict

**REJECTED.** 18/25 against v005's 20. The ratchet fires. No hard veto fires — including the grind veto, which I have cleared on the merits and ruled on for good.

Say plainly what this rejection is and is not.

It is **not** a judgement that the game got worse. It did not. `marla.stage.2` fires for the first time in six versions, `bodkin.stage.2` and `pim.stage.2` with it, the metrics file contains two instruments it has needed for three versions, and the grind number can finally fail. Nothing broke, nothing became unreachable, no error appeared. On any reading of the artifacts v006 is a slightly better game than v005.

It is **not** the grind veto. I cleared that above and I would have cleared it whatever the count said.

The rejection is arithmetic, and it is arithmetic on two conditions that were published in advance, in plain language, on the two axes this version's own work was aimed at. Condition 1 said A falls to 3 if the arc is still ~16 in-game days, "whatever the reason" — wording chosen specifically to close the deferral defence, in a version that then deferred. Condition 2 said D does not hold 4 on 14 unique lines with zero references to player-made state — and 14 unique lines with zero references is exactly what arrived. Both conditions were achievable, both were about the artifact rather than the history, and neither is the kind of trap this series has twice been right to void: v003's `worstAbsoluteRepetition < 55` was arithmetically unsatisfiable alongside the item it was paired with, and v004's `shareOfWindow ≤ 0.20` was calibrated against a baseline the loop had already deleted. **Nothing is wrong with these two.** Voiding them would be pure convenience for the developer, and an arbiter that voids conditions when honouring them would be inconvenient is not an arbiter.

**Now the part that matters more than the verdict. Do not revert the queue.**

The lesson this series should have learned from v004 is not "don't ship big changes", it is "don't answer a rejection by deleting the work". v004's back half was the largest structural improvement in the series, it was reverted whole for two points lost on unrelated axes, and three versions later the game still has a 16-day arc because of it. The queue is in exactly that position now: it is the right mechanism, it demonstrably works, it is simply aimed at one third of the problem. Reverting it would cost the series its second-best structural asset for the second time and would put `marla.stage.2` back to 4-of-5 for a seventh version. **Keep it. Extend it to triggering.** What v006 got wrong is not the queue; it is that the developer treated a delivery fix as a fix for a triggering problem, and then shipped without checking the two families it was supposed to serve.

**And on whether deferring the back half is now the dominant problem: it is the largest absence, but it is not the dominant problem, and v007 should not lead with it.** The reason is mechanical rather than diplomatic. Every back-half milestone will need to say something when it fires — that is the only way it reaches pillar 3 and the only way it avoids being v004's twenty silent thresholds again. This version has just demonstrated that reactions still do not reach the player: the `*.room.*` family is worse, the `*.home.*` family is unmoved, and the sample names nothing. Building 280 days of content on top of a reaction system in that state reproduces v004 exactly, and the ratchet will delete it exactly the same way. Finish the reaction path first — it is one version of work and it is already two-thirds built — then ship the back half **with** its dialogue and its screenshots in one version, as v005 said and as v004's corpse demonstrates.

## The single most important weakness

**The reaction system was rebuilt to fix delivery when two of its three families were failing at triggering, and the version shipped without checking the two families it was aimed at.** The evidence is in three places and it converges. *The stage family proves the queue works:* `marla.stage.2` went from missed by 4 of 5 seeds to missed by 1, and `bodkin.stage.2` and `pim.stage.2` moved with it, closing a five-version-old supersession defect exactly as designed. *The other two families prove the diagnosis was incomplete:* `*.home.*` is byte-identical to v005 (`marla.home.feather` 2 of 5, `marla.home.reed` 2 of 5, `bodkin.home.glassbead` 1 of 5) and `*.room.*` is worse, with all three `*.room.water` lines now missed by 4 of 5 seeds. *And the `*.room.water` misses name the mechanism outright:* seed 7 sees all three of them and seeds 101, 2024, 55555 and 888001 see none of the three — a perfect correlation that means one gate, not three misses. The fix is one pass over the trigger conditions rather than the delivery path: re-key `*.room.*` from a single narrow world-state condition to something every playthrough reaches, re-key `*.home.*` from named scarce items to categories any furnished room will satisfy, and — separately and cheaply — make the dialogue sampler draw from what the queue actually delivered, because a sample crossing `decorated`, `home:full` and six builds that contains zero references to any of them is not evidence about the writing, it is evidence that the artifact is not looking where the feature is. Success is measurable in fields already in the file and needs no new instrumentation: no defined line missed by more than **one** seed in five (currently five lines missed by four), minimum per-seed `dialoguePct` ≥ **97** (currently 93.8), Marla at ≥ **3** of 14 sample slots (currently 1), and ≥ **3** of 14 sample lines naming something the player gathered, placed or built (currently 0, for the third consecutive version).
